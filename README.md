# Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) Analysis - 2024

**Verification Email:** 24f1002577@ds.study.iitm.ac.in

## Business Context

The company is a financial services firm. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) represents the average expense incurred to acquire a new customer. Monitoring CAC is critical for evaluating marketing efficiency and overall profitability.

## Quarterly CAC Data

| Quarter | CAC ($) |
|---------|---------|
| Q1      | 225.38  |
| Q2      | 232.30  |
| Q3      | 231.35  |
| Q4      | 229.83  |

**Average CAC for 2024:** 229.72

**Industry Benchmark:** 150

## Key Findings

- The CAC has remained consistently high across all quarters, averaging **229.72**, which is **significantly above the industry benchmark of 150**.
- There is modest quarter-to-quarter variation; the lowest CAC is Q1 (225.38) and the highest is Q2 (232.30).
- The persistent gap to the industry benchmark indicates that the current marketing channel mix and conversion funnels should be optimized urgently.

## Business Implications

- A high CAC compared to the industry benchmark indicates suboptimal marketing efficiency and will compress ROI unless addressed.
- If CAC remains above the industry benchmark, the company must either increase customer lifetime value (LTV) or reduce acquisition spend to maintain margins.
- Strategic reallocation of marketing budget and improved attribution will be necessary to efficiently lower CAC.

## Recommendations

1. **Solution — "optimize digital marketing channels"**: Review and optimize the digital channel mix. Reduce investment in high-cost/low-conversion campaigns and scale channels with lower CAC and higher LTV (search, organic, referrals). Test bidding strategies and creative/landing page variants to improve conversion rates.
2. **Refine Customer Targeting**: Use customer segmentation and analytics to focus on high-potential prospects and reduce wasted impressions.
3. **Leverage Referral and Organic Programs**: Referral programs and organic growth typically have much lower CAC; run a pilot referral program with capped incentives.
4. **Experiment and Measure**: Implement systematic A/B tests on landing pages, creatives, and audiences, and measure cost per conversion by cohort.
5. **Improve Attribution & Tracking**: Ensure accurate attribution to properly credit channels and stop/scale campaigns based on true cost per acquisition.

Additional tactical steps to help reach the target of 150:

- Reallocate at least 20% of paid spend to lower-cost channels and referral pilots for a 6-month experiment.
- Negotiate performance-based pricing with agencies and ad platforms where feasible.
- Track CAC by channel weekly and run monthly reviews to catch regressions early.

5. **Negotiate Vendor Contracts**: Work with marketing service providers to optimize costs and explore performance-based pricing models.
